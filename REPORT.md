## Decisions and remaining gaps

1. The legal route takes 6¾–7¾ minutes to reach the lot. The 2,499 m drive, its full stops and the allowable Baker–Bay–Palace approach take priority over the 3–4 minute target. The demo uses the requested 25 mph street limit. The source data retains Chestnut's mapped 20 mph segment.
2. The parked view gets the rotunda through the open colonnade, with part of the dome and the lagoon visible. Near columns and their crossbeam still cover much of it compared with the open composition in `rv-07`. The physical upper-dome reflection falls on dry ground from the parked eye. The visible reflections are lower architectural fragments and warm streaks. The lot's four free bays fit a simplified loop and there is no moving NPC traffic inside it.
3. Chestnut's flatter street and modular façades, plain glass and low-poly pedestrians are visibly simpler than `rv-01`. The scene has fewer groups of moving cars directly in front of the player than either night reference. Distant hills, dock berths and sculpture details are approximations.
4. The road reads 2–5 blue–red codes less blue than `rv-01`, and the headlight patch contrast is lower than the phase 1.6.1 matched frame. Warm pools remain around lit stores. The Marina Boulevard look photo is daylight, and the night bridge photo comes from nearby Crissy Field.

Run `heavy node "tools/convert-splats.mjs" "tools/splat-lot.json"` from the repo root. A new street scene can supply its own JSON config. Set the source directory, static GLB, bake manifest and output directory. Define each group with a node-name expression, spacing in metres, width multiplier and count allowance. Individual objects can override the group spacing. Omit the parked-car adapter for a street whose static vehicles already live in the GLB. Choose a new output directory for a new scene.

The converter places deterministic area-weighted samples on transformed triangles. It decodes UV0 colour textures and the UV1 irradiance atlas to linear light, applies the per-instance atlas rectangle, material and vertex colours, the mesh's lightmap intensity and Lambert factor, then adds emission and the existing night exposure shoulder. Vehicle colours use their paint variants, five directional parking probes and interpolated surface normals. The sampling approach credits the Hall of the Goblet converter and Mert Cobanov’s MIT-licensed work in the converter source. The generator rejects leaf-mask samples below the mesh cutoff. Dark materials have a small per-group ambient term calibrated against the display. Colours go to Spark as photographic sRGB. Each RAD file contains aligned 65,536-splat chunks with deflated property planes, float centres, eight-bit colour and opacity, and half-float rotations and log scales. Spark builds display LODs at load time.

Spark **2.3.1** uses the existing three.js **0.183.2**. The tablet, full-screen picture, windscreen and rear camera each have an independent radial sort and LOD selection. They share one set of loaded assets. The colour pass uses the opaque meshes' depth buffer. A separate depth-only draw for the two Reality View targets supplies coverage for the sharp bay overlays. The immutable lot data and original camera occluders continue to drive the simulation.
